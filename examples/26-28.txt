## Problem 12
(a) The force due to the electric field is distinguished from that associated with the magnetic field in that the latter vanishes when the speed is zero and the former is independent of speed. The graph shows that the force (y-component) is negative at $ v=0$  (specifically, its value is $ -2.0×10^{-19} N$  there), which (because $ q=-e$ ) implies that the electric field points in the $ +y$  direction. Its magnitude is
$ E = \frac{F_{net,y}}{|q|} = \frac{2.0×10^{-19} N}{1.6×10^{-19} C} = 1.25 N/C = 1.25 V/m$ 

(b) We are told that the x and z components of the force remain zero throughout the motion, implying that the electron continues to move along the x axis, even though magnetic forces generally cause the paths of charged particles to curve. The exception to this is when the forces due to the electric and magnetic fields cancel. This implies (Eq. 28-7) $ B = E/v = 2.50×10^{-2} T$ . For $ \vec{F}_B = q\vec{v}×\vec{B}$  to be in the opposite direction of $ \vec{F}_E = q\vec{E}$ , we must have $ \vec{B}$  in the opposite direction from $ \vec{v}×\vec{E}$ . Since $ \vec{E}$  points in the $ +y$  direction and the velocity is in the $ +x$  direction, then (using the right-hand rule) we conclude that the magnetic field must point in the $ +z$  direction ($ \hat{i}×\hat{k}=-\hat{j}$ ). In unit-vector notation, we have $ \vec{B} = (2.50×10^{-2} T)\hat{k}$
